# ADR-017: Simulator stock is a dexel model, cut where it runs fastest

## Status
DRAFT

## Context

The image-space CSG simulator redraws every cut each frame, so it slows as a **Job** goes on, and it drops walls where a cut lies under earlier cuts. Collisions with the **Workholding**, **Tool Holder** and shank need a stock model that can be queried. FreeCAD runs on an OpenGL 2.1 compatibility context on macOS, and on machines from a 4-core laptop with a large graphics card to a 32-thread desktop with a small one.

Options considered:

1. **Keep image-space CSG.** No new code; the slowdown and missing walls stay.
2. **A voxel or Z-map model** (like the old simulator). Cannot show undercuts.
3. **A dexel model:** rays along X, Y and Z, each holding the stretches of material it crosses, meshed for display.

## Decision

The stock is a dexel model (option 3), meshed by dual contouring.

- Shaders stay GLSL 1.20, so macOS runs it; a compute-shader backend may come later behind a capability check.
- The stock is cut on the CPU or the graphics card. One setting, "Simulator runs on: Auto / CPU / GPU", on the Advanced preferences page. Auto runs a one-second benchmark the first time the simulator opens on a computer, kept per CPU and graphics driver, and picks the faster side that fits the card's memory. No hardware is assumed.
- Collisions are checked where the stock is cut.
- The simulator stops at each collision and marks it on the timeline.

## Consequences

- Gains: undercuts and collisions are exact; the frame rate does not fall as a **Job** goes on.
- Costs: memory per stock grows with resolution; macOS and Windows are not yet tested.
- Open: a fixture plate or table as a single plane rather than a dexel grid.
